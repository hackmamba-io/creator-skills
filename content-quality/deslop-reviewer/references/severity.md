# Severity classification

Group findings by severity so the writer fixes the loudest tells first. A draft with three High violations reads as AI-written no matter how clean the rest is.

## High — instant AI tells, fix first

These are the patterns a developer spots in the first paragraph and immediately distrusts.

- W2 · Contrast framing
- W5 · Marketing language
- W6 · Generic openings
- W7 · Narrated code
- W10 · Paired adjectives
- W13 · Topic-announcement openers
- W14 · Fake inclusivity
- W15 · Manufactured stakes
- W16 · Conclusion recaps
- W18 · Excited-to-announce openers
- W25 · Triple lists of abstractions

## Medium — weakens the writing

Not always an obvious tell, but each one makes the prose flatter and less trustworthy.

- W1 · Filler phrases
- W3 · Em-dash overuse
- W4 · Rhetorical questions
- W8 · Passive voice
- W9 · Hedging
- W11 · Meta-references
- W12 · Mechanical transitions
- W17 · Faux-conversational pivots
- W19 · Repetitive sentence starters
- W21 · Corporate clichés
- W22 · Scare quotes
- W23 · Bold emphasis in body copy
- W24 · Emoji as emphasis
- W26 · Uncontracted forms
- W30 · Repeated thematic points

## Low — statistical, matters in aggregate

Any single instance is harmless. Flag these when they cluster.

- W20 · "very"
- W27 · Typographic quotes and dashes
- W28 · Repetitive distinctive words
- W29 · Sentence-length uniformity

## Reporting format

Report High first, then Medium, then Low. Within each tier, list violations in the order they appear in the draft so the writer can work top to bottom. Close with the tally:

> **7 violations across 5 of 30 checks** — 3 High, 3 Medium, 1 Low.
